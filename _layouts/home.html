Welcome Note

We are glad to welcome you back to Mind Matters 2026, our Young Adolescent Peer Educators Leadership Conclave. To mark World Mental Health Month, we will hold discussions and workshops on 30 and 31 October. There will also be stall exhibitions and inter-school competitions, and we will be working with mental health institutes and organisations, among other events. We hope to see you on both days.

This year there are 11 competitions, including new ones such as Drishti, Manakaavya, and The Mentalist. They give participants room to be creative and express themselves, and to learn about mental health by taking part.

From the Principal's Desk

Ms. Anitha Bijesh,
Principal - DPS Bangalore South

It gives me immense pleasure to welcome you to Mind Matters – the Mental Health Conclave, an initiative that continues to strengthen our commitment to the emotional well-being of young people.

At DPS Bangalore South, we believe that mental well-being must move beyond awareness to become a shared culture of understanding, empathy and action.

Mind Matters seeks to create just such a community of practice - bringing together students, educators and experts to listen, learn, exchange perspectives and collectively build more compassionate school communities. At the heart of the conclave lies our commitment to providing every child with a voice and a meaningful platform for expression. Through thoughtfully curated competitions and creative platforms, students are encouraged to explore themes of well-being, resilience, relationships, belonging and self-expression. Whether through words, art, performance, dialogue or ideas, these spaces enable young people not merely to participate in conversations about mental health, but to shape them.

The conclave is also an opportunity to learn from one another - because meaningful change occurs when individual experiences become shared understanding and shared understanding translates into collective responsibility.

As we embark upon our third edition, I hope Mind Matters continues to grow as a platform where voices find expression, experiences find acceptance, and conversations inspire action. For when we create communities that listen with empathy and respond with sensitivity, we do more than support well-being - we empower our children to become advocates for themselves and for one another.

With warm regards,
Anitha Bijesh
Principal

About the Conclave

At Delhi Public School Bangalore South, we believe awareness and curiosity come from practical experience. Mind Matters was conceptualised in 2023, initiated by the Peer Educators to help others become aware and break the stigma against mental health to create and reiterate the need for practical experience.

Our conclave features include a range of challenging, thought provoking activities and a series of interactive workshops that help students to reflect, collaborate, step out of their comfort zones; all while learning about stress management, empathy, emotional wellbeing and a chance to experience their talent.

All the events in Mind Matters are centred around mental health to create awareness, break stigma built against it. Our goal is to create a community where every student feels heard, understood and empowered.

Who can participate?

  • Enterprising and talented high school students from your school or institute from classes VIII, IX, X, XI, XII, along with first-year and second-year college students, can participate in this event.
  • Groups of delegates from different institutes are welcome to participate in the event.
  • Teacher Coordinators and Counsellors from participating schools/institutions
  • Participants who have passion and true commitment towards raising awareness and promoting mental health within the school community and outside.
  • Students with effective communication and leadership skills.
  • Students who are interested in gaining more knowledge about life skills and mental health topics.

About the Peer Educator Programme

Peer Educators are mental health ambassadors who work to make our school more aware, empathetic and supportive. We want students to be able to talk about mental health without embarrassment, to look after their emotional well-being, and to feel seen and heard in a school that is safe and inclusive. We run awareness initiatives, hold conversations, and work with others to challenge misconceptions and encourage students to ask for help. Looking after your mental health matters as much as looking after your physical health.

The programme started in 2021, and we are still building a school culture based on empathy, acceptance and understanding. Even one small conversation can change something, and every student deserves support. Join us as we Engage, Enable, Empower, and Evolve, so that mental health matters here and no one feels alone.
